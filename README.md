## How to apply an EveryPlate discount code (and why codes fail)

The redemption flow itself is simple:

1. Choose your plan (people × recipes per week).
2. Continue to the Select Your Plan / checkout page.
3. Paste the code into the "Do you have a discount?" box and click Apply.
4. Wait for the confirmation message — "Your Discount Has Been Applied" — before paying.

That fourth step is the one that saves people money. The confirmation tells you the offer is attached to your account and plan, not just advertised in a banner. If a code silently does nothing, it's usually for one of three reasons: it's restricted to new customers, it's tied to reactivating a canceled account rather than a live one, or your selected plan doesn't meet the offer's conditions. ## The billing trap: how skipping and cancellation actually work

EveryPlate runs an auto-renewing weekly subscription, and the mechanics matter more here than with most products because the deadlines are tight.

- Orders are billed automatically each week, and **an order locks 5 days before its delivery date**. Miss the window and that box ships and charges regardless.
- Skipping a week is free and unlimited — do it in the "My Deliveries" page, and you can skip multiple consecutive weeks without any penalty.
- Cancelling is the same: do it at least 5 days before the next scheduled delivery, and you're done.

The practical takeaway: if you sign up, put a recurring reminder on your calendar a week out from your first delivery. That's the decision point — keep the subscription, skip a week, or cancel before anything else charges. People who get burned by meal-kit subscriptions almost always got burned at this step, not at checkout.

## Who should skip EveryPlate entirely

EveryPlate keeps costs down by stripping out things some customers need, and it's cheaper to know that upfront.

- **Anyone with dietary restrictions.** MealFan's 2026 review — based on 24 meals across 8 boxes — is blunt: no vegan, keto, paleo, or gluten-free track. EveryPlate serves meat-and-potatoes cooking to people who eat meat and potatoes. The weekly menu preview is viewable online without giving your email, so check a couple of weeks of menus before subscribing if you're a picky eater.
- **Anyone with a genuinely empty kitchen.** The box contains portioned proteins, produce, and sauces, but recipes assume you own oil, salt, pepper, and often butter or eggs. Those don't come in the box. Most kitchens have this stuff; if yours doesn't, stock up before your first delivery or you'll stop mid-recipe.
- **Solo diners who hate leftovers.** The smallest plan is 2 people × 3 recipes. The standard workaround — treat the second serving as next day's lunch — works well for some people and not at all for others.
- **Absolute bottom-dollar hunters.** EveryPlate isn't the cheapest meal kit on the market (more on that below), though the gap is smaller than list prices suggest.
